Version 2: what was built

Version 1 of this document was the design. The team approved it with eight technique changes (docs/DECISIONS.md, decisions 12 to 18), two system changes (a web API and a test console, decisions 21 and 25) and one removal (no AI or LLM features, decision 23). This section says what exists now. Where the rest of the document and this section disagree, this section and docs/API.md win.

How it runs

  • Backend: a FastAPI service on the team laptop (api/), bound to 127.0.0.1 and reached through a Tailscale Funnel tunnel with a shared bearer token. The agent (src/agent/) runs inside it.
  • Frontend: a Next.js test console on Vercel (web/), one page per module. The browser never calls the backend; the passcode protects every change. With the laptop off it shows saved results.
  • No command-line agent. The commands in "API Contracts" became web endpoints, listed in docs/API.md. The experiments are scripts: run_experiment.py and run_benchmarks.py.

What was measured

  • Ridgecrest: QAOA's final pick was the exact optimum in 15 of 15 runs. Chance per measurement 5.1% to 11.0% for 1 to 3 layers. Simulated location error 3.00 km with no new sensors, 2.63 km expert rule, 2.31 km our pick.
  • Five benchmark earthquakes: CVaR made the final pick exact in 14 of 15 runs (8 of 15 without). The 20% target (AC-7) was missed in all runs.
  • AC-16: the best-scoring network beat the expert rule on 1 of 5 earthquakes in the simulated location test (Ridgecrest, 2.4%); on Puerto Rico it was 15.8% worse. The D-criterion does not always predict location error.

Sources: results/results.json, results/benchmarks.json, research/benchmarks/2026-11-12_benchmark_results.md.

Context

The problem

After a large earthquake, response teams install portable seismometers to record the aftershocks. In the first hours, the instruments that matter most are the few that send data in real time. Thousands of cheap nodal sensors may follow later, but those typically record to internal memory and are collected weeks afterwards. Where the first real-time instruments go decides how sharply aftershocks can be located while the emergency is still unfolding.

Methods for choosing optimal station sites have been published since the 1970s–90s:

  • Kijko (1977), early optimal network planning;
  • Rabinowitz & Steinberg (1990), the D-optimality criterion;
  • Hardt & Scherbaum (1994), aftershock networks designed from the mainshock alone.

Published accounts of real deployments still describe siting qualitatively. For example, the USGS at Ridgecrest in 2019 aimed to "improve azimuthal coverage and provide additional near-field observations" (Cochran et al. 2020). We found no tool that recommends sites automatically after an earthquake. Existing tools are either for designing a network in advance (AuScope Seismic Network Design App) or for judging an existing network (SN-CAST). Both claims rest on short descriptions and must be re-checked (R-1).

Why the published methods stay on paper, and how we respond

Barrier (evidence in research/) Response in this design
Computed ideal spots are often unusable (access, permits, noise, safety) Candidates are built from reachable places, each with backup spots (FR-6, FR-7, FR-21)
Cheap dense nodes reduce scarcity Scope limited to the first few real-time instruments (Out of Scope, OS-1)
A sensible human layout may be nearly as good An explicit expert-rule baseline, with the gain over it reported honestly (FR-26, AC-16)
Results depend on an assumed Earth model Sites are scored across several velocity models (FR-10)
Teams want more than location accuracy Scope stated as location accuracy only (OS-3)
Aftershocks migrate Hourly re-planning (FR-23 to FR-25)
No tool and no expert time during a crisis The autonomous agent itself (FR-1 to FR-5)
Field teams distrust black boxes Every site comes with its reason and score (FR-21)

What the proof of concept already showed

On the real Ridgecrest 2019 data (commit d34e328):

  • The quantum circuit (Dicke state plus XY-mixer QAOA) found the exact optimum in 15 of 15 runs.
  • Per-shot success probability was 5.1–10.0%, against 0.48% for a random valid network.
  • The pairwise score tracked the true score with Spearman ρ = 0.987.
  • Predicted blur on later aftershocks fell from 2.73 km to 1.50 km.

Known weaknesses that this design must fix:

  • the approximate score,
  • grid-only candidate sites,
  • a single velocity model,
  • no human-style baseline,
  • one earthquake only,
  • predicted rather than tested accuracy.

Positioning

This is a proof of concept. The full pipeline runs end to end on real data, with the quantum step simulated in Qiskit. No quantum speedup is claimed: with 210 valid networks, classical search finds the same answer instantly. The contribution is an autonomous, tested pipeline whose decision step is a quantum optimisation algorithm, evaluated honestly against exact and classical references.

Planning stages within one earthquake

Stage When Aftershocks used Purpose
T0 plan Within 5 minutes of trigger Synthetic, generated from the mainshock (Hardt & Scherbaum 1994) A first answer before real aftershocks accumulate
Hourly plans Every 60 min Real catalogue events so far (plus synthetic events while fewer than 30 real ones exist) Refine as data arrives
Re-plan When drift is detected Real events Recommend moves, priced by a moving cost

Where the quantum computer is used

Only in step 5. It decides which 4 of 10 candidate sites to use, at every T0, hourly and re-plan decision. All other steps are classical: data, physics, candidate generation, scoring, checking, reporting and memory. The quantum step is the decision-maker, and its output is always checked against the exact optimum (step 6). How often the quantum step alone finds the optimum is logged and reported (FR-18).

Functional Requirements

Trigger and data

  • FR-1 The agent MUST poll the USGS real-time GeoJSON feed at least once per minute in live mode and start a run for each new event with magnitude ≥ 6.5 that has at least one permanent station within 100 km of its epicentre (in practice, onshore or near-shore events).
  • FR-2 The agent MUST support a replay mode that runs any past earthquake as if live, using catalogue data up to a simulated clock time and never data after it.
  • FR-3 The agent MUST download the aftershocks recorded so far (USGS ComCat FDSN event service, M ≥ 2.5, within a radius R = max(50 km, 2 × rupture length) of the epicentre).
  • FR-4 The agent MUST download permanent stations within radius R that are operating at the event time (FDSN station service).
  • FR-5 The agent MUST cache every download with its source URL and retrieval time, so that every run can be reproduced offline.

Candidate sites

  • FR-6 The agent MUST build a pool of reachable candidate points:
    • a 2 km grid covering radius R;
    • kept only if within 1.5 km of a road (OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API);
    • not inside water;
    • at least 6 km from any permanent station;
    • at least 3 km from the mapped rupture trace (crew safety).
  • FR-7 For each candidate the agent MUST keep up to 3 backup points from the pool, within 3 km and passing the same filters.
  • FR-8 The agent MUST select the final N = 10 candidates from the pool by greedy spread selection. It repeatedly adds the point with the highest single-site score gain, subject to a minimum spacing of 4 km between selected candidates. (This pre-selection is classical; its effect on the final answer is measured in AC-17.)
  • FR-9 The team MUST be able to add or exclude candidate points through a config file before or during a run.

Scoring

  • FR-10 The score of a network S MUST be the D-criterion averaged over an ensemble of velocity models V (default: uniform 5.5, 6.0 and 6.5 km/s, plus one regional layered model where available), with weights w_v from memory (equal weights on first use): D(S) = Σ_v w_v Σ_e log det F_e,v(P ∪ S)
  • FR-11 The objective MUST be f(S) = D(S) − D(∅) − λ_risk Σ_{i∈S} c_i − λ_move · moves(S).
    • c_i is the access risk of site i: exp(−d_i / 5 km), where d_i is the distance to the nearest aftershock.
    • moves(S) is the number of sensors that change site compared with the current plan (0 for a first plan).
  • FR-12 The agent MUST compute the exact expansion of f over all subsets of up to 4 sites (the Möbius coefficients; 385 terms for N = 10). Inside the 4-site subspace the resulting Hamiltonian then equals f exactly, with no approximation.
  • FR-13 The agent MUST convert the expansion into a Qiskit SparsePauliOp with Z, ZZ, ZZZ and ZZZZ terms, normalised so that its largest coefficient has magnitude 1.

Quantum decision step

  • FR-14 The agent MUST choose sites with QAOA in Qiskit:
    • initial state: the Dicke state |D(10,4)⟩, prepared with the Bärtschi & Eidenbenz (2019) circuit;
    • each layer: a cost layer exp(−iγH), then an XY ring mixer of XXPlusYYGate in parity order;
    • p layers, with p starting at 3.
  • FR-15 Parameters MUST be optimised with COBYLA on the CVaR-α of the energy distribution (Barkoutsos et al. 2020; α = 0.1), i.e. the mean energy of the best 10% of outcomes, computed exactly from the statevector probabilities. The plain mean energy is not used.
  • FR-16 The initial angles MUST come from the angle library in memory (median of the stored optimal angles for the same N, k and p). If memory is empty, the agent MUST use 4 random restarts.
  • FR-17 After optimisation the agent MUST sample 1,024 shots with StatevectorSampler. It then re-scores the 10 most frequent networks with f and keeps the best.
  • FR-18 The agent MUST log, for every quantum decision: p, number of evaluations, P(opt) per shot, the rank of the chosen network, and runtime.
  • FR-19 The agent MAY run a reduced instance (N = 6, k = 2) on IBM quantum hardware through qiskit-ibm-runtime, as a separate demonstration whose results are reported but not used for planning.

Self-check and publish

  • FR-20 The agent MUST compute the exact optimum by enumeration (210 subsets). If the quantum choice differs from it, the agent MUST retry with p + 2 layers and 4,096 shots, at most twice. If the quantum step still disagrees, the agent MUST publish the exact optimum and flag quantum_agreed = false in the plan.
  • FR-21 The agent MUST publish:
    • plan.json (API Contracts);
    • map.png, showing aftershocks, permanent stations, candidates, chosen sites and backups;
    • plan.txt, a plain-language summary. For each site it gives coordinates, backups, reason (its single-site gain and redundancy with the other chosen sites), risk, and expected location improvement.
  • FR-22 The plan MUST include a confidence level computed from the agent's prediction-error record (FR-28): the error of a past plan is |realised score − predicted score| / predicted score, and the level is "high" if the median past error is ≤ 25%, "medium" if ≤ 50%, otherwise "low". It is "unrated" when there is no record.

Monitoring and re-planning

  • FR-23 After publishing, the agent MUST re-check every 60 minutes for 72 hours.
  • FR-24 The agent MUST declare drift when either:
    • the centroid of aftershocks from the last 6 hours has moved more than 5 km from the centroid used for the current plan; or
    • the realised score of the current plan on the new aftershocks is more than 20% below its predicted score.
  • FR-25 On drift, the agent MUST re-plan (steps 4–7) with λ_move > 0 and publish either explicit moves ("move sensor from site A to B") or "stay".

Learning across earthquakes

  • FR-26 The agent MUST compare every plan against four reference strategies, all scored with the same objective f:
    • exact optimum;
    • greedy forward selection;
    • simulated annealing (Hardt & Scherbaum 1994 style);
    • an expert rule: pick sites in turn to minimise the largest azimuthal gap around the aftershock centroid, breaking ties by distance to the centroid (closer first). This mirrors the published qualitative practice.
  • FR-27 At the end of a sequence the agent MUST store, in memory:
    • the optimal QAOA angles for (N, k, p);
    • the realised performance of each velocity model;
    • the predicted and realised plan scores.
  • FR-28 The agent MUST update the velocity-model weights w_v in proportion to each model's realised accuracy over all past sequences, smoothed by Laplace smoothing with equal priors.
  • FR-29 The agent MUST expose a leave-one-out evaluation command. It trains the memory on all benchmark earthquakes except one, replays the held-out one, and repeats for every earthquake.

Non-Functional Requirements

  • NFR-1 Speed: a full plan (steps 2–7, with cached data, N = 10, k = 4, p = 3) MUST finish within 5 minutes on the reference laptop (Intel i7-1265U, 32 GB RAM).
  • NFR-2 Reproducibility: with the same cache, config and seed, two runs MUST produce identical plans and metrics.
  • NFR-3 Resilience: every network call MUST have a timeout of 30 s and 3 retries with exponential backoff. Failures fall back to the cache, or to the behaviour listed under Edge Cases.
  • NFR-4 Correctness of the Hamiltonian: the diagonal of H restricted to weight-4 states MUST equal the normalised f within 1e-9.
  • NFR-5 Constraint safety: no measurement from the main quantum circuit may have Hamming weight other than 4.
  • NFR-6 Test suite: automated tests MUST run in under 60 s with cached data and no network access.
  • NFR-7 No secrets: live and replay modes MUST work without API keys. IBM hardware access (FR-19) reads its token from an environment variable and is never committed.
  • NFR-8 Honest reporting: every reported metric MUST be produced by a script in the repository from cached data. No hand-entered numbers.

Edge Cases

  • EC-1 USGS feed unreachable: retry per NFR-3, log, and keep polling. No run starts without a confirmed event.
  • EC-2 No real aftershocks yet: use synthetic aftershocks (T0 plan).
  • EC-3 Fewer than 30 real aftershocks: blend real and synthetic events.
  • EC-4 Epicentre offshore, or fewer than 10 reachable candidates: lower N to the number available (minimum 6) and state it in the plan. Below 6 candidates, publish "insufficient reachable sites" and stop.
  • EC-5 Overpass API unavailable: build candidates from the grid only, mark feasibility_checked = false and show a prominent warning in plan.txt.
  • EC-6 No focal mechanism available yet: generate synthetic aftershocks on both nodal planes. If there is still none, use a circular cloud with radius equal to half the rupture length.
  • EC-7 The mainshock magnitude is revised by USGS: if the change is ≥ 0.3, re-run from step 2.
  • EC-8 A second qualifying event within radius R during monitoring: merge it into the same sequence and declare drift.
  • EC-9 Quantum step disagrees after retries: handled by FR-20.
  • EC-10 Memory empty (first ever run): random restarts, equal model weights, confidence "unrated".
  • EC-11 A requested k ≥ N, or k < 1: reject with a clear error message.

Robustness tests used in the evaluation (AC-15)

  1. Held-out aftershocks: blur measured on aftershocks after the planning time.
  2. Model mismatch: travel times generated with a layered regional model plus 0.1 s noise, then located with the agent's ensemble using the chosen network. The measured error is reported in km. This is the closest test to "real" error possible for stations that were never installed.
  3. Real deployment comparison (where station metadata exist, R-3): the agent's network against the stations the field team actually installed, both scored on the same held-out aftershocks.

Out of Scope

  • OS-1 Placing large nodal arrays (hundreds of sensors). The agent targets the first k ≤ 6 real-time instruments.
  • OS-2 Processing seismometer data (picking, locating with real waveforms). Existing ML pickers do this.
  • OS-3 Goals other than location accuracy: fault imaging, strong-motion recording, site response.
  • OS-4 Field logistics: travel routing, crew scheduling, permits.
  • OS-5 Claims of quantum speedup, or quantum hardware at planning scale. Hardware appears only as the FR-19 demonstration.
  • OS-6 Notifying real agencies. Outputs are shown on the team's web console and stored on the team laptop.
  • OS-7 Earthquakes outside regions covered by USGS ComCat at M ≥ 2.5.

Architecture

Version 2: the same modular monolith, laid out as src/ (science), src/agent/ (the agent), api/ (FastAPI) and web/ (Next.js console). docs/ARCHITECTURE.md describes hosting and security.

Style: modular monolith in one Python package (quakenet/). A small team, one runtime and a batch-style loop do not justify services. Every module is pure and testable except sources (network) and memory (files), which sit behind interfaces so replay and tests can substitute cached versions.

Boundary conditions. This design holds while N ≤ 12 (exact enumeration and exact expansion stay cheap). Above that, the exact expansion grows combinatorially, so the objective must fall back to second- or third-order terms, and the self-check must use classical heuristics instead of enumeration. The objective and self_check interfaces are designed to allow that change without touching the quantum or agent modules.

Trade-offs accepted.

  • Statevector simulation limits N to about 25.
  • A deeper cost layer, with 4-body terms, makes hardware execution unrealistic.
  • The pre-selection to 10 candidates is classical, and its effect is measured (AC-17) instead of assumed away.

Risks and Open Checks

  • R-1 Verify what the AuScope Network Design App and SN-CAST can do (inputs, live data, automation) before claiming the gap. This affects the problem statement. Done; problem statement changed (decision 11).
  • R-2 Confirm that each benchmark earthquake has enough M ≥ 2.5 aftershocks in the first 12 h and accessible station metadata. Replace any that fail. Done; South Napa 2014 has too few later aftershocks and is skipped.
  • R-3 Find station metadata for real temporary deployments (for example, the USGS Ridgecrest 2019 network). Without it, robustness test 3 is dropped and the report says so.
  • R-4 Verify that USGS event pages provide focal mechanisms quickly enough for T0 plans. Otherwise EC-6 applies.
  • R-5 Verify the claim that nodal sensors typically do not transmit in real time, with sources, before it appears in the pitch.
  • R-6 CVaR plus warm start may not reach the 20% P(opt) target. The report states whatever is achieved. Happened: best was about 10% per measurement.

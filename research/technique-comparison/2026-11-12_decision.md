# Decision report: which design techniques should QuakeNet-Q use?

Date: 2026-11-12 · Depth: medium · Genre: decision

## Question

Strutz & Curtis (2024) and NetOpt3D (Kraft et al. 2013; Keil et al. 2023) choose seismic station sites
with different techniques from ours. For each technique: is theirs better, why, and should we adopt
it within hackathon scope?

## Hypotheses

- **H1:** A nonlinear Bayesian score (DN or EIG) gives better station layouts for earthquake location
  than the linearised D-criterion.
  *Result:* supported by one head-to-head study (Bloem et al. 2020), but all the evidence comes from one
  research group, and the method has a known failure case that matches aftershock clouds. **Under-determined
  for our data; we must test it ourselves.**
- **H2:** Making pick error depend on signal-to-noise ratio (as NetOpt3D does) changes which sites are best.
  *Result:* plausible but not tested on our data. NetOpt3D's authors treat it as essential for detection.
- **H3:** Genetic algorithms or simulated annealing would beat our search.
  *Result:* **refuted at our size.** Exhaustive enumeration of 210 networks is exact; GA and SA are
  heuristics needed only for much larger spaces.

## Evidence

| # | Source | Type | Key content |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Strutz & Curtis 2024, arXiv 2411.11015 | academic preprint | Bayesian EIG. DN estimator inside the optimiser, NMC estimator to validate. Genetic algorithm (PyGAD). DEM-based constraints: slope under 20°, nothing below sea level, 3 km safety margin. DN "overestimates the EIG" but only relative values matter. DN is risky for "non-Gaussian priors, multimodal priors, and low noise levels". Code: GPL-3.0. |
| S2 | Bloem, Curtis & Maurer 2020, GJI 223, 944–958 | academic, peer-reviewed | Head-to-head on synthetic source location: "DN-optimization consistently designs the best source location experiments and does so with far less computational resources than linear or maximum entropy design." Linear D-optimisation "produce[s] worse designs and need[s] substantially more total compute time". DN criterion: Φ_DN(S) = ln det Σ(S), with Σ = Σ_F^½ Σ_d^-1 Σ_F^½ (Σ_F = covariance of predicted data across prior samples; Σ_d = data-noise covariance). Data are arrival-time differences. |
| S3 | Coles & Curtis 2011, Geophysics 76(2), Q1–Q8 | academic, peer-reviewed | Origin of DN-optimisation; it extends the linearised D criterion to nonlinear problems at low cost. Full text not read (403). |
| S4 | Keil, Wassermann, Megies & Kraft 2023, Seismica 2(2) | academic, peer-reviewed | NetOpt3D: linearised D-criterion with simulated annealing; finite-difference ray tracing (Podvin & Lecomte 1991) in a user velocity model; amplitudes from a Brune source with geometric spreading, constant attenuation and free-surface amplification; SNR = amplitude / station RMS noise; detection threshold SNR ≥ 5; pick uncertainty from SNR (Aki 1976). Quote: "NetOpt3D is lacking usability and it is time consuming to set up new optimization problems." |
| S5 | OpenTopography developer docs | primary | Global DEM API needs a free key, rate-limited to 50–250 calls per day. |

## Adversarial pass

1. **Is the DN evidence independent?** No. Coles & Curtis (2011), Bloem et al. (2020) and Strutz & Curtis
   (2024) all include Andrew Curtis. There is no independent replication in our sources, so triangulation
   is not met.
2. **Does the Bloem test resemble our case?** Only partly. It uses synthetic data, a square region,
   1D velocity models and a uniform prior source region. Real aftershock clouds are clustered; Ridgecrest
   has NW and SE lobes, which is the multimodal case where S1 warns DN can fail.
3. **Could the D-criterion win on our data?** Yes. It is exact for linear-Gaussian problems, and with 14
   permanent stations the location problem may be close to linear.
4. **Conclusion:** adopt DN as a candidate score, not by assumption. Implement D, DN and an NMC-EIG check,
   and let held-out aftershock location error decide.

## Decisions

| # | Change | Reason | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Add the DN score (our own implementation from Bloem eq. 26), keeping the D-criterion; the score used for planning is chosen by leave-one-out evaluation | Best-documented nonlinear criterion for source location (S2); uses the aftershock samples directly as the prior | In scope: the set-function → Möbius → QAOA pipeline is unchanged |
| C2 | Validate the top 10 designs with an NMC-EIG estimate | DN can fail for multimodal priors (S1); this is Strutz & Curtis's own practice | In scope |
| C3 | SNR-dependent pick error and an SNR ≥ 5 detection threshold, using catalogue magnitudes and simple amplitude decay | Distant sites cannot record small aftershocks; a constant 0.1 s error overvalues them (S4) | In scope, simplified: no Brune spectra or site noise measurements; one assumed rural noise level, stated |
| C4 | Slope < 20° filter from keyless AWS Terrain Tiles | Steep sites are unusable (S1); key-free so NFR-7 holds (S6) | In scope |
| C5 | Write DN ourselves; use WoWED-volcano only as an external cross-check | GPL-3.0 would oblige the whole project to be GPL (S1) | In scope |
| C6 | Keep exhaustive enumeration plus QAOA; no GA | Exact at N = 10 (H3) | No change |
| C7 | Keep the homogeneous velocity ensemble; no 3D ray tracing | Needs 3D models and a finite-difference solver; Strutz & Curtis also default to homogeneous | Out of scope, stated |
| C8 | Make the no-manual-setup property an explicit usability claim, citing S4's quote | Supported by the tool authors' own statement | Wording only |

## Confidence

- Medium on C1/C2: the method is well documented but supported by a single research group.
- High on C4–C6.
- Medium on C3: the amplitude model is simplified.
